## 2. Visual design system

The site uses warm off-white, deep navy and a vivid blue. Keep Instagram recognizable as the same company while making every post legible on a phone.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Main background | Warm off-white `#FCFCFA` |
| Main text / dark panels | Deep navy `#0B1B33` |
| Primary accent / CTA | Blue `#2563EB` |
| Pale panels | Light blue `#EFF5FF` |
| Typeface | Plus Jakarta Sans; if unavailable in the editor, use a clean geometric sans such as Inter |
| Headline | Bold, short, high contrast; 6–10 words per cover where possible |
| Support text | Regular/medium weight, large enough to read without zooming |
| Logo | Small KadaVerse mark/wordmark in the same corner on each design |
| 3D elements | Use real KadaVerse product and client screens inside tilted browser and phone frames. Keep the depth in the device edges, overlaps, lighting and orbit motif; avoid unrelated stock 3D objects. |
| Images | Use real product screens, approved client work, team/founder photos, diagrams and whiteboard sketches. Never imply a concept illustration is a real client result. |

### Reusable layouts

1. **Carousel cover:** off-white background; small blue eyebrow label; editorial headline; layered browser and phone mock-ups showing approved client work. Inside slides use one point per slide, generous margins, a small progress marker and a simple diagram or product screenshot.
2. **Reel cover:** short hook in large type; pair one clear message with tilted phone mock-ups of real product screens and a simple checklist card. Keep headline and CTA centered safely for the grid crop.
3. **Case study:** dark navy cover with client/project title; inside sequence: situation → what was built → how the workflow works → invitation to discuss a similar problem. Use only approved screenshots and do not add unverified impact claims.
4. **Story card:** vertical light background, one question, layered real product screens and three numbered choices. The starter card invites replies with 1, 2 or 3; use a native Poll or Quiz sticker on other Story prompts in the calendar.

The refreshed starter set is in `instagram-assets/`. Ready-to-upload PNGs and matching SVGs are available for the website carousel cover, Reel cover and Story question card. The SVGs include the branded mark and real screen images. Use the carousel's slide copy in the calendar below to carry its visual style through the full set.

### Production rules

- Feed carousels: design at **1080 × 1350 px (4:5)**. Keep text and logos well inside the edges.
- Reels and Stories: design at **1080 × 1920 px (9:16)**. Keep essential text out of the top and bottom interface areas.
- Use burned-in captions on Reels. Start with the useful point in the first seconds; cut pauses; end with one clear CTA.
- Keep one main idea per post. Use screen recordings with demo data or client-approved, privacy-safe screens.
- Add image alt text where available. Check contrast and spellings before scheduling.
- Use a location tag when it is relevant. Use a small group of specific hashtags (brand + service + audience/location); do not paste the same broad hashtag block onto every post.

## 3. Posting rhythm and measurement

**Feed:** Monday Reel, Wednesday carousel, Friday Reel each week. 12 feed posts across four full weeks.  
**Stories:** one to three frames most days; use polls, question stickers, work-in-progress clips and feed-post follow-ups.  
**Initial posting-time experiment:** Monday/Friday Reels at **7:00 pm IST**, Wednesday carousels at **12:30 pm IST**. These are test slots, not guaranteed best times. After two weeks, compare reach, saves, shares, profile visits and qualified enquiries in Instagram Insights, then adjust. Instagram’s Professional Dashboard includes personalized Best Practices guidance, so check it as the account gathers data. [Meta’s Best Practices announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/)

Use original screen recordings and founder/team footage. If available on the account, Trial Reels can help test a new hook with non-followers before sharing it more broadly. [Meta’s Trial Reels guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/)

Track once a week:

- **Discovery:** reach, non-follower reach, Reel watch/retention where available.
- **Useful interest:** saves, shares, replies, profile visits.
- **Business outcome:** relevant DMs, enquiry-page clicks, qualified calls booked, proposals requested.
- **Learning:** which audience/problem/hook produced a real conversation—not just views.

## 6. Caption and community workflow

### Before publishing

1. Confirm the post solves one audience problem and has one CTA.
2. Get client permission for names, logos, screenshots, quotes and recordings.
3. Remove private data and verify every factual claim.
4. Check mobile crop, captions, audio rights, links, spelling and alt text.
5. Add location and a few relevant tags where useful; do not stuff unrelated hashtags.
6. Schedule the post and prepare a Story that adds a poll, question or explanation.

### After publishing

- Respond to comments and DMs within one working day.
- If someone is interested, ask: “What are you trying to improve, who uses the current process, and when are you hoping to solve it?”
- With their agreement, move the conversation into the normal lead tracker and schedule a discovery call.
- Do not diagnose a complex system or provide a full unpaid specification in DMs.
- Add the lead source as Instagram so you can tell whether content creates real project conversations.

### Monthly review

At the end of the 30 days, record each post's reach, saves, shares, replies, profile visits, link taps, relevant DMs and calls booked. Compare like with like (Reels against Reels, carousels against carousels). Repeat topics that create useful conversations; change hooks or formats when people drop off.
